Each draw holds the reported vote counts fixed. It samples remaining turnout from unit lognormal turnout priors with a common shock, then allocates the outstanding vote with a logistic-normal share model. Completed reporting units update statewide and regional log-ratio swing using a normal hierarchical approximation. The observation variance includes a between-unit floor. Partial units contribute their counted votes, and only completed units inform the geographic swing fit. Every region must contain a completed unit with votes before the engine produces an estimate. This is a coverage check, and it cannot correct systematic differences between completed and outstanding units.

The simulator supports plurality contests with two to twelve named candidates. Margins are for a fixed candidate pair declared before replay. Votes are allocated continuously in the simulation, which approximates the remaining count without adding per-ballot noise. The default is 20,000 draws per distinct supported input. Finite-run endpoints are smoothed unless every unit has been explicitly marked complete. Tied outcomes split a draw among tied candidates. The interval is the simulated 10th to 90th percentile, pending empirical coverage validation.

The default `full-coverage` method still abstains if any region lacks a completed unit. The experimental `partial-pool` method allows missing regional completion evidence when at least two distinct regions have completed units. An unobserved region borrows the statewide posterior and retains its regional variance. Both methods require as-of candidate counts for the full geography and reconciliation to the race totals. The `frozen-baseline` comparator preserves reported votes and documented completion, then uses pre-election distributions alone for the remaining ballots. This isolates the value added by the completed-unit swing update. The DDHQ election-night paper supports using completed units to learn swings. The FEA fallback itself still needs held-out validation.

## Running a replay

Run `node scripts/results/probability-replay.mjs <frozen-race-bundle.json>`. A bundle supplies:

- `prior` with `raceId`, `office`, `rule: "plurality"`, `electionDate`, `availableAt`, `source`, stable `candidateIds`, a two-ID `marginCandidateIds` array, and a complete `units` array. Each unit has `id`, `region`, `turnout`, `turnoutLogSd`, and candidate-keyed `shares`. The prior must be frozen before the election date.
- `snapshots` in strictly increasing observed-time order. Each has `raceId`, `at`, `sourceUpdatedAt`, `source`, candidate-keyed race `totals`, and all reporting `units`. Every unit supplies `id`, `availableAt`, `complete`, and candidate-keyed integer `votes`. A complete unit must include its `completionSource`. A decrease in counts or reversal of a completion flag requires `correctionSource`.
- Optional `settings` for research simulations. All timestamps must include the time zone. Source timestamps must be backed by contemporaneous artifacts. For a genuinely observed zero, include explicit evidence of zero. A missing unit is not zero.

Research points are stored under `win-probability/reconstructed/<model-version>/<method>/<race-id>/` inside the configured results storage. The replay preserves corrections as new points, skips unchanged input, and rejects conflicting writes at an existing timestamp. `scoreProbabilityReplay` takes outcomes separately after simulation and returns multiclass Brier score, log loss, margin error, interval coverage, and probability reliability bins using one point per race and reporting-stage decile.

Run `node scripts/results/probability-benchmark.mjs <holdout-bundles.json> <certified-outcomes.json>` with `{policy,bundles}` and separately held truth keyed by `electionDate:raceId`. Truth supplies `winner`, `margin`, `marginCandidateIds`, `totalVotes`, and `source`. The policy predeclares `method`, `frozenAt`, `holdoutDates`, `cohorts` (office and stage decile), `minimumRaces`, `minimumCycles`, `coverageTolerance`, and model `settings`. It reports matched and available checkpoints and compares paired Brier score, log loss, margin error, and 80% coverage. Retrospective reporting strata use final totals solely for evaluation. A successful screen still requires independent review of actual preregistration, provenance, calibration, and performance uncertainty. A date in a JSON file alone does not establish preregistration.

### Outstanding ballot limits

Research snapshots can include an `outstandingLimit` on a reporting unit. The record must give `raceId`, `electionDate`, `unitId`, `scope: "all-ballots"`, `reportedVotes` keyed by candidate, a nonnegative integer `maxBallots`, `availableAt`, and the issuing `source`. The counts must match the unit exactly. The limit must be available by the observed snapshot time and cover all ballot classes that could still contribute votes. An estimate of votes counted or a count of only one outstanding ballot class is insufficient. A limit caps the outstanding vote in each simulation and does not qualify the unit as completed.

Use a separate benchmark with `method: "outstanding-limits"` to compare capped and uncapped full-coverage forecasts at identical checkpoints and with identical random draws. A total bound contradicted by certified turnout invalidates the benchmark input. The production release gate does not accept this research method. Validation requires archived, dated all-ballot limits and matched count snapshots across held-out election cycles.

The Ohio Secretary of State's November 4, 2026 09:06 UTC election-night XML snapshot lists 3,779 outstanding absentee and 98,369 outstanding provisional ballots across the 88 counties, even though all 88 showed every precinct reporting. The XML snapshot was marked `UnofficialPartial`. The listed categories were not used as a verified all-ballot limit. [Ohio election-night data](https://liveresults.ohiosos.gov/)

Before a nationwide release, the background county fetch must also sustain the five-minute freshness requirement. An earlier one-race-per-15-second budget would have revisited 506 races in about 126 minutes. The live scheduler now refreshes up to three eligible NBC county pages every 1.5 seconds while reserving one request slot for public race details, for a nominal ~254-second full sweep when requests complete on time. Race-total configurations skip county hydration. On November 6, 2026, the NBC national payloads exposed 506 distinct race-page URLs for the 506 mapped races. The current adapter requires each page's update endpoint to match its page path, so a shared state feed cannot be assumed from that inventory. Increasing the freshness window would not establish that the estimates are current; Render throughput at full geographic scope still needs live measurement.

### Aggregate count replay without a county feed

The [2020 timestamped count archive](https://github.com/d0ntw84f8/election2020-timeseries-data/tree/main/house-senate-president) includes 434 House and 33 Senate contests in the first two calendar days after polls closed. The study checks the CSV checksum and joins to FEA's checked-in final-vote files. It uses the count's own timestamp, drops repeated observations and conflicting rows, and excludes unsupported election rules, ambiguous major-party matchups, and counts inconsistent with final returns. It retained 341 House and 22 Senate races. Each comparison uses the first checkpoint per race per time band.

Run `node scripts/results/probability-count-study.mjs <archive.csv> <turnout-study.json>`. The second file is the report from `probability-turnout-study.mjs`, which checks the original capture checksums for the 2022 and 2024 Senate races. The aggregate method samples historical changes in the reported D–R margin, weighting each race by similarity in elapsed time and current margin. The output estimates which major-party candidate finishes ahead of the other. It cannot represent independent winners or non-plurality outcomes.

For the 2020 House sample, leaving out the target state during fitting gave Brier scores of .147, .064, and .029 across the three windows, versus .167, .066, and .038 for a baseline giving the counted leader a 90% chance. The 80% margin interval covered 73%, 84%, and 71% of actual margins. In the separate 2022 Senate cycle the model lost to the counted-leader baseline in both tested windows (.080 vs .010 across 3 races and .029 vs .010 across 5). Only three 2024 Senate races qualified for the elapsed-time window. The House result warrants a later-cycle test. It does not establish skill outside 2020.

The [AP 2024 snapshot archive](https://github.com/cuihaoleo/2024-us-election-result-snapshots) supplies an independent House and Senate test. The checked-in manifest pins 15 archive commits and hashes both files from each. Retrieve `progress.json` and `metadata.json` from each commit into a local research directory as `probability-ap2024-<commit>-<file>.json`. Append `data/results/probability-ap2024-manifest.json <capture-directory>` to the command above. The loader verifies hashes, the official race and candidate identities, vote totals, and the timing of the count relative to poll close and the archived frame. It uses no call metadata.

The AP test retained 285 House races and 22 Senate races. The count-only method was compared with both the fixed leader probability and a logistic probability fitted to 2020 margins, separately by office and window. The House model beat the fitted baseline in the first five hours of the AP sample (.095 vs .112 Brier, 212 races), but lost on the 33 races with a counted margin within 10 points (.266 vs .244). On days 6–14 it improved over the fitted baseline in 174 AP House races (.0112 vs .0131), including 35 with counted margins within 10 points (.056 vs .065). Its 80% interval covered 77% in that window. The same late House method lost to the fitted baseline in the 2020 held-out-state test (.0070 vs .0056). No band currently supports release across cycles, and governor and multiway winner probabilities are outside this experiment.

A blend of the two probabilities was also fitted on the 2020 state-held-out predictions, then applied with fixed weights to the later cycles. The blend improved the first 2024 House window relative to the fitted margin baseline (.100 vs .112 Brier), trailed the baseline through the next morning (.042 vs .041), and selected the baseline alone on days 3–14. It did not show a consistent improvement.

New national race history points preserve their NBC or CivicAPI provider labels. Old points without a provider remain unlabelled, including when the current race is sourced from NBC. Forecast validation should use explicitly sourced points or independently matched archives.

### Statewide fallback when a turnout estimate is missing

Run `node scripts/results/probability-statewide-study.mjs <AP 2024 manifest> <NYT 2022 manifest> <capture directory> <governor returns CSV> <NYT 2020 manifest>`. The governor CSV is [FiveThirtyEight's election results transcription](https://github.com/fivethirtyeight/election-results/blob/main/election_results_gubernatorial.csv). Only rows that cite a state government source are eligible. Independently reviewed FEA state canvasses take precedence where available. The official [2012 FEC turnout table](https://www.fec.gov/documents/1683/2012pres.pdf) contributes 51 state and DC rows that sum to 129,085,410. The 2016 and 2020 anchors use the FEC returns already in the repository.

The fallback uses the last presidential turnout, scaled by earlier certified Senate or governor turnout for midterm or presidential cycles. Its vote-margin signal retains uncertainty even after a race exceeds historical expected turnout. It ignores the current provider's estimated turnout. The model is trained on the earlier cycle with the scored state withheld. The resulting Brier score evaluates which of the two major-party candidates finishes ahead. It cannot establish complete winner probabilities for multi-candidate or special-rule races.

In the 60–89% historical-turnout stage, Senate's combined model scored .053 Brier against .065 prior-only and .061 count-only on 16 held-out 2022 contests, and .052 against .054 and .060 on 22 held-out 2024 contests. Governor's combined model improved on both baselines at 25–59% in both tests, with only 11 and 8 contests respectively. Later governor results were mixed.
